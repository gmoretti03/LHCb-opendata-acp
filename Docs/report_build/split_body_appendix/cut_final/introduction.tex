\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The observable of this work is the raw charge asymmetry of the signal yields of $B^{\pm}\to K^{\pm}K^{+}K^{-}$ decays,
\begin{equation}
\Araw=\frac{N(B^-)-N(B^+)}{N(B^-)+N(B^+)} ,
\label{eq:araw}
\end{equation}
which in three-body charmless $B$ decays can be studied globally and locally over the Dalitz plane~\cite{lhcb_cpv_prd2023,lhcb_cpv_paper}. It differs from the $CP$ asymmetry $\ACP$ by the production asymmetry $A_P$ of $B^\pm$ in $pp$ collisions and by the detection asymmetry $A_D$ of the final state~\cite{lhcb_cpv_prd2023},
\begin{equation}
\ACP\approx\Araw-A_P-A_D .
\label{eq:acp}
\end{equation}
Neither correction is applied here, so $\Araw$ is \emph{not} a measurement of $\ACP$; the corrections of the published analysis are discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison_published}.
We use the LHCb Open Data record for this decay~\cite{opendata_record,opendata_notebook}, a subset of the 2011 data on which the first evidence of an inclusive $CP$ asymmetry was published~\cite{lhcb_cpv_2013}, to ask: \emph{to what extent do Frequentist likelihood fits and Bayesian inference produce consistent, well-controlled estimates of $\Araw$?} The aim is a methodological comparison of the two frameworks on the same data and on the same likelihood, so that any difference comes from the inference and not from the model. Secs.~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:model} describe the data and the common model, Secs.~\ref{sec:frequentist}--\ref{sec:bayesian} the two fits, and Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} the validation of the estimator and of the intervals, Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_body} the machine-learning analysis, Sec.~\ref{sec:dalitz} the Dalitz plane, Sec.~\ref{sec:syst} the systematic study and the result, and Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison} the comparison. The main results are in Table~\ref{tab:summary}.
