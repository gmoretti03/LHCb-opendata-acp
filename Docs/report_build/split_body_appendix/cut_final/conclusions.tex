\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
\label{sec:comparison}
\emph{In short: the Frequentist and Bayesian analyses give the same $\Araw=-0.044\pm0.009\,(\text{stat})\pm0.007\,(\text{syst})$, $5.2$ standard deviations from zero, with validated intervals; they differ in the meaning of the numbers and of the tests, not in their values.}
On this problem the Frequentist profile-likelihood fit and the Bayesian posterior are equivalent in value, uncertainty and coverage: with a common selection and mass model they give $\Araw=-0.0441\pm0.0085\pm0.0072$ and $-0.0443\pm0.0085\pm0.0070$ (statistical, systematic), a nonzero asymmetry at $5.2$ standard deviations, stable under the thirteen variants (the dominant systematic contribution is the particle-identification requirement) and concentrated in the Dalitz region of the published analysis outside the $\phi(1020)$ ($-0.234\pm0.025$ against $-0.239\pm0.020$).
\textbf{The numbers coincide, the statements do not.} The \emph{credible interval} states that $A$ lies in $[-0.0528,-0.0358]$ with $68\%$ probability given the data and the prior; the \emph{confidence interval} $[-0.0526,-0.0356]$ is the set of values that the data do not reject, and its $68\%$ is a property of the procedure that the bootstrap and the toy experiments verified\appref{app:cmp_meaning}. The Frequentist test of $A=0$, $p=2\times10^{-7}$, is independent of the prior; the Bayes factor, $BF_{10}\approx9\times10^3$, depends on its width (Sec.~\ref{sec:bayes_factor}), and for $A_b=-0.040\pm0.018$ a Frequentist test would call $2.3\sigma$ a hint while $BF_{01}\approx3.5$ mildly favors $A_b=0$. The Frequentist fit adds an unbiased estimator at the minimum variance bound; the Bayesian analysis adds sampling diagnostics and a posterior predictive check. Figure~\ref{fig:checks_profile} shows the profile likelihood and the marginal posterior of $A$ on the same axis, and the likelihoods of the two magnet polarities.

\label{sec:comparison_published}
The published analysis~\cite{lhcb_cpv_2013} finds $-0.060\pm0.007$ on a larger sample with a richer model\appref{app:cmp_pubsamples}; with the same $D^0$ veto the Bayesian fit gives $-0.0496\pm0.0088$, within $0.93\sigma$. The raw asymmetry is not the $CP$ asymmetry, $\ACP\approx\Araw-A_P-A_D$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:acp}): the $5.2\sigma$ here is the significance of a nonzero raw asymmetry, not of $CP$ violation. Three limitations bound the result\appref{sec:limitations}: no tested model reaches $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}\approx1$, although $\Araw$ does not depend on the signal shape; no production or detection correction is applied; and the local Dalitz-plane asymmetries are counts without background subtraction. A machine-learning selection leaves $\Araw$ unchanged within its statistical spread (Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_body}), and the natural next step is a control-channel correction.
\subsection{Additional Results}
\emph{Numerical agreement.} The estimates of the two fits differ by $0.0002$, both uncertainties are $0.0085$, and sideband subtraction gives $-0.0503\pm0.0083$\appref{sec:comparison_numbers}.
\emph{Profile, posterior and polarities.} Profiling and marginalizing the eleven nuisance parameters give the same distribution of $A$, since $A$ is almost uncorrelated with them; the two polarities agree and their combined likelihood reproduces the joint fit; the $68\%$ and $95\%$ regions in $(A,A_b)$ of the profile ($\Delta\mathrm{NLL}$ at $2.30$ and $5.99$) and of the highest posterior density coincide, with $A=0$ outside both\appfigs{sec:checks_profile}{Table~\ref{tab:checks_polarity}}{sec:checks_region}{Fig.~\ref{fig:checks_region}}.
\emph{Tests and goodness of fit.} The exact binomial test of $N^-=N^+$ on all $19{,}731$ candidates ($A_{\mathrm{count}}=-0.0432\pm0.0071$) gives $6.1\sigma$, larger than the $5.2\sigma$ of the fit ($-0.0441\pm0.0085$), and the two are not in tension: the count tests the charge asymmetry of signal and background together, without the mass, so its uncertainty does not include the cost of separating the background, while the fit tests the asymmetry of the signal alone with that of the background free; the $5.2\sigma$ is the one that refers to the signal\appref{app:cmp_test}. The same fit has $p=0.03$ in $2.5\,\MeV$ bins and $p\approx5\times10^{-6}$ in $10\,\MeV$ bins: the $p$-value of the binned $\chi^2$ depends on the binning as much as on the model\appref{app:cmp_gof}.
\emph{Low statistics.} Credible and confidence intervals coincide in value and coverage from $\sim2\times10^4$ down to $50$ candidates\appfig{sec:bayes_lowstat}{Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes_lowstat}}.
