\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Charge-parity (CP) violation -- the small observed asymmetry between the behavior of matter and antimatter -- is one of the necessary ingredients for the matter-dominated universe we observe, yet the CP violation built into the Standard Model is far too small to account for it. Studying CP violation in the decays of $B$ mesons, where it is comparatively large and experimentally accessible, is one of the main ways to test the Standard Model's Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) description of the effect and search for physics beyond it. Charmless three-body decays such as $B^{\pm}\to h^{\pm}h^{+}h^{-}$ ($h=K,\pi$) are especially interesting in this respect: unlike a two-body decay, the three-body phase space (the Dalitz plot) allows the asymmetry to be studied not only as a single global number, but also \emph{locally}, since interference between intermediate resonances can concentrate CP-violating effects in specific kinematic regions rather than spreading them uniformly~\cite{lhcb_cpv_prd2023,lhcb_cpv_paper}.

The quantity measured in this work is the raw asymmetry between the signal yields of the two charges,
\begin{equation}
\Araw=\frac{N(B^-)-N(B^+)}{N(B^-)+N(B^+)} .
\label{eq:araw}
\end{equation}
It differs from the physical asymmetry $\ACP$ by the production asymmetry $A_P$ of $B^\pm$ mesons in $pp$ collisions and by the detection asymmetry $A_D$ of the final state; to first order in these small quantities,
\begin{equation}
\ACP\approx\Araw-A_P-A_D ,
\label{eq:acp}
\end{equation}
where $A_D$ arises mainly from the different interaction cross sections of $K^+$ and $K^-$ with the detector material and depends on the kaon momenta~\cite{lhcb_cpv_prd2023}. Neither correction is applied here, so $\Araw$ is not a measurement of $\ACP$. The production asymmetry of $B^\pm$ mesons at the LHC is expected at the level of one per cent; in the published analysis of the same 2011 data, the two corrections, measured with $B^\pm\to J/\psi K^\pm$ decays, together with an acceptance correction, move the asymmetry of $B^\pm\to K^\pm K^+K^-$ from $\Araw=-0.060\pm0.007$ to $\ACP=-0.043\pm0.009\,(\text{stat})\pm0.003\,(\text{syst})\pm0.007\,(J/\psi K)$~\cite{lhcb_cpv_2013}.

In this work we use the LHCb Open Data record for $B^{\pm}\to K^{\pm}K^{+}K^{-}$~\cite{opendata_record,opendata_notebook}, a subset of the 2011 data on which the first evidence of an inclusive $CP$ asymmetry in this decay was published~\cite{lhcb_cpv_2013}, to address the question: \emph{to what extent do frequentist likelihood fits and Bayesian inference produce consistent, well-controlled estimates of the raw asymmetry $\Araw$ in this decay?} The aim is not a competitive measurement of $\ACP$, but a methodological comparison of the two inference frameworks on the same data and the same physical question. Of the four channels in the record, $B^\pm\to K^\pm K^+K^-$ has the lowest background; the others ($\pi^\pm\pi^+\pi^-$, $K^\pm\pi^+\pi^-$, $\pi^\pm K^+K^-$) need a different particle-identification selection and a model of larger and peaking (misidentified) backgrounds, and are not analysed, to keep the focus on the comparison of the methods. The published $\ACP$ cannot be reproduced from this record, which does not contain the control channel used to correct $\Araw$ for the production and detection asymmetries (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}); what we extend is the statistical treatment: a common model fitted with two inference methods, a common systematic study, a validation of the intervals, and a machine-learning (ML) selection, described in Appendix~\ref{sec:ml}, whose effect on the measurement is tested. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the dataset and the preselection common to the analyses and Sec.~\ref{sec:model} the mass model and likelihood common to the two mass fits; Secs.~\ref{sec:frequentist} and \ref{sec:bayesian} present the frequentist and Bayesian fits, Sec.~\ref{sec:syst} their common systematic study, Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} the validation of the estimator and of the intervals with pseudo-experiments, Sec.~\ref{sec:dalitz} the local asymmetry in the Dalitz plane, and Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison} compares the two approaches; the machine-learning selection is in Appendix~\ref{sec:ml}.
