\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
Several modeling choices and known weaknesses limit how far the results of Secs.~\ref{sec:bayesian}--\ref{sec:ml} can be pushed, and are collected here rather than scattered through the results sections.

On the mass fits (Secs.~\ref{sec:bayesian}--\ref{sec:syst}): no tested signal model reaches $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}\approx1$ (the best, a Crystal Ball, gives $131.0/86$, Sec.~\ref{sec:bayes_gof}), although $\Araw$ does not depend on the signal shape; the systematic uncertainty, $0.007$, is almost as large as the statistical one; it is dominated by a PID variant that a finer scan identifies as a statistical fluctuation (Sec.~\ref{sec:syst}), so it is conservative, but the combination rule cannot separate the two; the Savage-Dickey Bayes factor of Sec.~\ref{sec:bayes_factor} relies on a Gaussian approximation of the posterior far in its tail and depends on the prior width, though the conclusion (decisive evidence against $A=0$) is robust; the toy-MC coverage check of Sec.~\ref{sec:bayes_toymc} is a closure test and cannot detect model misspecification; and the local Dalitz asymmetries (Secs.~\ref{sec:bayes_dalitz} and \ref{sec:ml_kmeans}) use a coarse partition, a mass-window cut rather than a proper background subtraction, and no correction for momentum-dependent detection asymmetries.

On the machine-learning side (Sec.~\ref{sec:ml}): the weak labels are imbalanced ($1940$ background-like vs.\ $15{,}130$ signal-like candidates, Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_labels}), and the test set contains only $388$ background-like candidates, so differences in test AUC below $\sim$0.01 cannot be resolved; the three uncertainty estimators differ by a factor $\sim$3.5 in scale and measure different sources of variability (Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_uncertainty}); and the score-based selection changes the composition of the background, keeping the partially reconstructed and other non-combinatorial $B$ decays that the nominal model does not describe (Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_stability}); removing the momentum features does not help, since the weak labels cannot separate these decays from the signal.

More generally, no detector-level systematic correction (selection efficiency, production asymmetry) is applied anywhere in this work, so $\Araw$ as reported throughout is explicitly \emph{not} a measurement of $\ACP$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}). The published analysis corrects for $A_P+A_D$ with $B^\pm\to J/\psi K^\pm$, $J/\psi\to\mu^+\mu^-$~\cite{lhcb_cpv_prd2023}, which is not part of this record; the $J/\psi\to K^+K^-$ decays inside our sample cannot replace it, since this mode is rare and the $568$ candidates under the $B$ peak in the $J/\psi$ band are dominated by charmless decays ($A_{\mathrm{count}}=-0.165\pm0.041$, a local Dalitz-plot asymmetry rather than a control measurement).

The most natural next steps, in rough priority order, are: correcting $\Araw$ for the production and detection asymmetries with a control channel such as $B^\pm\to J/\psi K^\pm$, $J/\psi\to\mu^+\mu^-$; replacing the fixed-cut local Dalitz asymmetries (both binned, Sec.~\ref{sec:bayes_dalitz}, and $K$-means, Sec.~\ref{sec:ml_kmeans}) with a proper sWeight-based background subtraction; and an explicit model of the partially reconstructed and other non-combinatorial $B$ backgrounds, which would also make an ML-selected sample usable for a fit.
