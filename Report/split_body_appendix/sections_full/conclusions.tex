\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
\emph{In short: two inference frameworks give the same raw asymmetry, $\Araw=-0.044\pm0.009\,(\text{stat})\pm0.007\,(\text{syst})$, $5.2$ standard deviations from zero, with validated intervals; what separates them is the meaning of the numbers and the tests, not their values; a machine-learning selection (Appendix~\ref{sec:ml}) confirms the result without improving it.}

We have measured the raw charge asymmetry of $B^\pm\to K^\pm K^+K^-$ decays in the 2011 LHCb Open Data record with a common selection and mass model, and compared how two inference frameworks treat the same question. The frequentist profile-likelihood fit and the Bayesian posterior give $\Araw=-0.0441\pm0.0085\pm0.0072$ and $-0.0443\pm0.0085\pm0.0070$ (statistical, systematic), a nonzero asymmetry at $5.2$ standard deviations, stable under the thirteen variants of the fit and selection, compatible in sign and size with the published analysis, and concentrated in its Dalitz region outside the $\phi(1020)$. It is a raw asymmetry, not a measurement of $\ACP$ and not a significance of CP violation (Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison_published}).

The methods differ in what they add: the frequentist fit an unbiased estimator at the minimum variance bound with intervals of nominal coverage; the Bayesian analysis the same interval with flat priors, plus sampling diagnostics, a Bayes factor that depends on the prior width and a posterior predictive check. The machine-learning selection leaves $\Araw$ unchanged. The dominant systematic contribution is the particle-identification requirement, in both methods. Three limitations bound the result (Appendix~\ref{sec:limitations}): no tested model reaches $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}\approx1$, although $\Araw$ does not depend on the signal shape; no production or detection correction is applied, so $\Araw\neq\ACP$; and the local Dalitz-plane asymmetries are counts without background subtraction by event weights.
