\begin{abstract}
We measure the raw charge asymmetry $\Araw=[N(B^-)-N(B^+)]/[N(B^-)+N(B^+)]$ of $B^{\pm}\to K^{\pm}K^{+}K^{-}$ decays in the 2011 LHCb Open Data record and compare three inference frameworks on the same data and the same question: an extended unbinned maximum-likelihood fit with profile likelihood, a Bayesian analysis sampled with Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), and a machine-learning-assisted selection. After a common preselection ($19{,}731$ candidates in the fit window), a common mass model gives $\Araw=-0.0441\pm0.0085\,(\text{stat})\pm0.0072\,(\text{syst})$ in the frequentist fit and $-0.0443\pm0.0085\pm0.0070$ in the Bayesian one, $5.2$ standard deviations from zero (Bayes factor $\approx9\times10^3$ against no asymmetry). The two methods agree in value, uncertainty and coverage, which was verified on pseudo-experiments; the dominant systematic uncertainty is the particle-identification requirement. The asymmetry is not uniform over the Dalitz plane: in the region of the published analysis of the same data we find $\Araw=-0.234\pm0.025$, reproducing $-0.239\pm0.020$. A machine-learning selection trained on weak labels leaves $\Araw$ unchanged within its statistical spread. $\Araw$ is not corrected for production and detection asymmetries and is therefore not a measurement of $\ACP$.
\end{abstract}
